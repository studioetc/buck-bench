History & the reconstruction

This is an educational reconstruction of Black Buck One, the first raid of 30 April–1 May 1982. The tanker relay and its failures are historical. The animation makes the sequence legible; it is not a navigation chart or an engineering fuel calculation.

WHY SOME NUMBERS DIFFER

Published summaries count aircraft, sorties and refuelling contacts differently. The RAF’s 2022 article mentions both 14 refuellings and 15 outward transfers, while other accounts describe seven outward Vulcan top-ups plus recovery. This animation uses the relay structure and seven outward top-ups described by the RAF Historical Society. Its on-screen transfer counter counts only the eight illustrated Vulcan top-ups; it does not claim a total for the entire operation. Elapsed times are reconstructed, since published departure and landing times also vary.

The mission

ASCENSION → FALKLANDS → ASCENSION
To send one bomber south,
send its fuel ahead.

Ascension Island was the launch point for a raid thousands of miles away. One Vulcan could not get to Port Stanley and back on its own fuel. Victors therefore carried fuel south, passed it into other Victors and the bomber, then turned home. At each handoff, the formation grew smaller.

The primary Vulcan withdrew after take-off. The reserve, XM607, continued. Farther south, a tanker probe broke in a storm. Bob Tuxford’s crew reversed the fuel transfer and took over the final leg, giving fuel from their own return margin. Both the bomber and that tanker depended on later rendezvous to get home.
