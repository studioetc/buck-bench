# Black Buck 01

Open `index.html` in a modern browser. It is a complete, self-contained HTML simulation: the coastline, fonts, styles and animation are embedded, so it also works offline. No installation or server is needed.

The geographic map follows the outward relay, storm damage, reversed tanker roles, final Vulcan handoff, tanker rescue and bomber recovery. **Expanded relay** spreads the aircraft into schematic lanes. The timeline supports scrubbing, chapter jumps, variable playback speed and pausing at historical events. Select an aircraft on the map, in the fleet strip, or in the inspector.

Keyboard: Space plays/pauses; arrow keys step five mission minutes; R restarts. Reduced-motion preferences start the simulation paused.

## Historical scope

This reconstructs Black Buck One, 30 April–1 May 1982. Documented events are separated from the illustrative model in the in-app **Sources & model** panel. Fuel percentages are an explanatory index, not measured aircraft fuel quantities; event times, paths and rendezvous positions are approximate. Early tanker identifiers and their transfer assignments are schematic. Recovery support is condensed. The original historical plan for Black Buck II is used only to understand the relay principle.

Primary research: RAF Historical Society Journal 30 (pp. 43–46), RAF Museum, RAF pilot recollections, and participant accounts collected by Michael Rondot. The simulation includes direct source links and explains disagreements in published aircraft, sortie and refuelling totals.

Coastline: public-domain Natural Earth, via World Atlas. Fonts: Barlow Condensed and IBM Plex Mono, under the SIL Open Font License.
