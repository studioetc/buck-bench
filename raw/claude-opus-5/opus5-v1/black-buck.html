SOURCES, MODEL & HONEST CAVEATS

This is a reconstruction, not a declassified plot. Below is exactly what is documented in open sources versus what I have modelled to make the relay legible.

Documented: treated as fact

  • Launch from Wideawake Airfield, Ascension Island at 2350Z on 30 April 1982: two Vulcans and eleven Victor K2 tankers, at one-minute intervals.
  • Formation was organised in three sections: RED (4 Victors), WHITE (4 Victors) and BLUE (3 Victors + 2 Vulcans).
  • Primary Vulcan XM598 (Sqn Ldr John Reeve) aborted shortly after takeoff with a failed cabin pressurisation seal. Reserve XM607 (Flt Lt Martin Withers) became the attacker.
  • The attacking Vulcan was refuelled seven times outbound and once on the return. The whole operation consumed roughly 15–17 Victor sorties and 18 air-to-air refuellings.
  • Vulcan fuel burn between the first and second brackets: 16,250 lb/hr.
  • Refuelling was flown at night, in radio silence, in formation, at 300 knots, probe-and-drogue.
  • Thunderstorms near 40° South in the final refuelling area. Sqn Ldr Steve Biglands' Victor XH669 snapped its refuelling probe while taking fuel from Sqn Ldr Bob Tuxford's XL189. The two swapped roles: XH669 gave its fuel back to XL189, and Tuxford pressed on past his planned turn-back point to fly the last bracket.
  • Tuxford delivered 7,000 lb less than planned. Withers left the last bracket with 7,000 lb instead of 14,000 lb of margin. Tuxford knew he would run dry roughly 500 nm short of Ascension, and broke radio silence to say so.
  • Attack: descent to 300 ft, climb to 10,000 ft for the bomb run at 330 kt, 21 × 1,000 lb bombs released in a stick angled across the runway. H2S radar fixed on Mount Usborne (2,313 ft), 33 miles west of Stanley. Escape was a 60° bank, 2 g turn. Success codeword SUPERFUSE at 0746Z.
  • One bomb cratered the runway. Withers recovered north, was helped onto his return tanker by a Nimrod, met Sqn Ldr Barry Neal's Victor off Brazil, and landed at Ascension at 1452Z, about 15 hr 45 min and 6,600 nm.
  • Victor serials used: XH669, XH672, XL162, XL163, XL188, XL189, XL192, XL232, XL511, XL513, XM717.

Modelled: reconstructed for legibility

  • Bracket positions and times. Open sources do not publish the seven bracket coordinates. I have spaced them along the great circle so that the Vulcan's documented 16,250 lb/hr burn exactly produces the documented 14,000 lb-planned / 7,000 lb-delivered outcome at the final bracket, and so the storm bracket falls at ~40°S as reported. They are plausible, not archival.
  • Serial-to-callsign mapping. Only XL189 (Tuxford) and XH669 (Biglands) are firmly attributed in open sources. The other nine serials are assigned to RED/WHITE/BLUE slots illustratively.
  • Fuel quantities other than the Vulcan's. Victor tank states and individual offload figures are reconstructed from aircraft capacities and cruise burn, then clamped so nothing goes negative. Treat the tanker bars as shape-of-the-problem, not ledger.
  • Formation spread is exaggerated. Real brackets were flown in tight formation; here the aircraft are fanned into lanes so that thirteen distinct tracks stay separable.
  • Routing is a great circle Ascension→Stanley. The real track was flown with weather and threat avoidance.
  • Day/night shading is a real solar-position calculation for 30 Apr–1 May 1982 (declination ≈ +14.9°), so the darkness over the target at bomb release is genuine, not decorative.
