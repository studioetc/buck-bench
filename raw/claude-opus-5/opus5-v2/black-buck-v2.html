THE FUEL CASCADE
Every aircraft is a lane. Lane thickness is the fuel in its tanks. Ribbons are transfers, sized by pounds moved. Read it left to right and the shape of the problem appears: fuel poured in at the top, cascading down through three sections of tankers, funnelling into one bomber that has to reach 51° South and come back.

SOURCES, MODEL & HONEST CAVEATS

A reconstruction, not a declassified plot. Here is what is documented against what I modelled.

Documented: treated as fact

  • Launch from Wideawake Airfield, Ascension at 2350Z, 30 April 1982: two Vulcans and eleven Victor K2 tankers at one-minute intervals.
  • Three sections: RED (4 Victors), WHITE (4 Victors), BLUE (3 Victors + 2 Vulcans).
  • Primary Vulcan XM598 (Sqn Ldr John Reeve) aborted early with a failed cabin pressurisation seal. Reserve XM607 (Flt Lt Martin Withers) took the raid.
  • The Vulcan was refuelled seven times outbound, once returning. The operation ran to roughly 15–17 Victor sorties and 18 refuellings.
  • Vulcan burn between brackets one and two: 16,250 lb/hr.
  • Refuelling flown at night, radio silent, in formation, at 300 knots, probe and drogue.
  • The Victor K2 tanked through a Mk17B centreline hose drum unit rated at 500 gal/min (roughly 4,000 lb/min) plus Mk20B wing pods at a much lower flow. It could feed one receiver on the centreline or two on the pods, but not both at once. The contact view obeys this.
  • Thunderstorms near 40° South in the last refuelling area. Sqn Ldr Steve Biglands' XH669 snapped its probe taking fuel from Sqn Ldr Bob Tuxford's XL189. They reversed roles: XH669 gave its fuel back so Tuxford could fly the final bracket.
  • Tuxford delivered 7,000 lb less than planned; Withers left with 7,000 lb of margin instead of 14,000. Tuxford knew he would run dry about 500 nm short of Ascension and broke radio silence.
  • Attack: down to 300 ft, up to 10,000 ft at 330 kt, 21 × 1,000 lb bombs in a stick angled across the runway. H2S fixed on Mount Usborne (2,313 ft), 33 miles west of Stanley. Escape at 60° bank, 2 g. Codeword SUPERFUSE, 0746Z.
  • One bomb cratered the runway. A Nimrod helped Withers find Sqn Ldr Barry Neal's tanker off Brazil. Landed 1452Z: about 15 hr 45 min, 6,600 nm.
  • Victor serials: XH669, XH672, XL162, XL163, XL188, XL189, XL192, XL232, XL511, XL513, XM717.
  • Vulcan B.2 ferry range about 4,000 nm, against a 6,800 nm round trip. That gap is the whole reason eleven tankers flew.

Modelled: reconstructed for legibility

  • Bracket positions and times. The seven bracket coordinates are not published. I spaced them along the great circle so the Vulcan's documented 16,250 lb/hr burn reproduces the documented 14,000-planned / 7,000-delivered outcome at the last bracket, and so the storm bracket lands at 40° South as reported. Plausible, not archival.
  • Serial to callsign mapping. Only XL189 (Tuxford) and XH669 (Biglands) are firmly attributed. The other nine are assigned to section slots illustratively.
  • Victor tank states and individual offloads are reconstructed from capacity and cruise burn, then clamped so nothing goes negative. Shape of the problem, not a ledger.
  • The contact view is schematic. Aircraft are not to relative scale or true separation, and hose length is exaggerated for legibility. Formation positions on the map are spread wider than reality so thirteen tracks stay separable.
  • Tanker reserves. Each Victor holds back enough fuel to reach Ascension from its planned turn-back point before it will give anything away. That rule is mine, not an archival one, but it is why XL189's arithmetic stops closing: Tuxford flew 52 minutes past the point his reserve was calculated for, so the shortfall falls out of the model rather than being typed in. It lands at 506 nm against the reported "roughly 500".
  • Routing is a clean great circle. The real track was flown around weather and threats.
  • Day and night shading is a genuine solar-position calculation for 30 Apr – 1 May 1982, so the darkness over the target at release is real rather than decorative.

30 APRIL 1982 · WIDEAWAKE AIRFIELD · ASCENSION ISLAND

One bomber.
Eleven tankers.
Nobody had tried this.

Port Stanley is 3,380 nautical miles south of Ascension. There is nothing in between. The Vulcan cannot make the round trip on its own fuel and cannot land anywhere at the far end, so every pound it burns has to be carried south and handed over in mid-air, in the dark, without radio.

That means tankers refuelling tankers refuelling tankers, each one turning back the moment it has given away everything it can spare. Get one contact wrong and the raid folds.
