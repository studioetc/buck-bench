OPERATION BLACK BUCK 1

WIDEAWAKE AIRFIELD, ASCENSION → PORT STANLEY · 30 APRIL – 1 MAY 1982

At 2350Z on 30 April 1982, eleven Handley Page Victor K.2 tankers and two Avro Vulcans began lifting off from Ascension Island at one-minute intervals. Their task: deliver a single Vulcan bomber — carrying 21 × 1,000 lb bombs — 3,300 nautical miles across the South Atlantic to strike Port Stanley's runway, and bring it home. No air force had ever attempted a bombing raid of this range.

Because the Victors had barely more range than the Vulcan, the tankers had to refuel each other in a relay, passing fuel south in a moving chain. The Vulcan was refuelled seven times outbound and once on the return. A failed window seal, a jammed refuelling probe in a thunderstorm, and a tanker flown home on fumes made this the most celebrated refuelling plan in aviation history.

This is a stylised real-time reconstruction. Tracks follow great circles between the documented refuelling brackets; timings, positions and fuel figures are approximations based on published accounts (Rowland White, Vulcan 607; Burden et al., Falklands: The Air War). Geography is generalised for readability.
