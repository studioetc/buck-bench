Black Buck fuel relay

To carry one Vulcan bomber to Stanley and back, the RAF built a moving pipeline in the sky. Victors refuelled Victors so that a fraction of their fuel could reach XM607.

The stream launches

Why the plan looks impossible

A tanker force feeding itself

The Victors could not simply escort the Vulcan to Stanley. They had to pass fuel forward in successive waves, turn north with barely enough to return, and leave fewer, more heavily fuelled aircraft continuing south.

Accuracy, reconstruction method & sources
This is a simulation, not a digitised flight log.

Public Black Buck 1 diagrams describe the transfer sequence but do not provide a complete, authoritative set of coordinates. The intermediate points here are placed along the Ascension–Stanley track using published distances and elapsed times. Aircraft lanes and offsets are deliberately exaggerated so the cascade remains readable.

Published totals vary because sources count different things: aircraft in the initial stream, air-to-air contacts, individual fuel transfers, Victor sorties, or the outbound and recovery waves together. This presentation uses 11 Victors for the main outbound stream, seven outbound Vulcan refuellings, and 18 Victor support sorties for the operation as reported by the RAF. The live fuel percentage is an illustrative model; the 73% fuel category is the remainder derived from the published 7% and 20% figures.
