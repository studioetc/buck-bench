About this reconstruction

This is an illustrative, research-grounded reconstruction of the air-to-air refuelling plan behind Black Buck 1, the RAF's first Vulcan raid of the 1982 Falklands War — at the time the longest-ranged bombing mission in history. It is built from multiple secondary historical accounts, cross-checked against each other, plus real geographic data for the coastlines and airfield coordinates.

What's solid vs. reconstructed: Aircraft serials, crew names, takeoff time (2350Z), the XM598 abort, the Biglands probe failure, the Tuxford fuel emergency, the 0746Z "Superfuse" success signal, the 35° offset bomb run, and the 1452Z landing are all drawn directly from cited sources. The exact geographic position and minute-by-minute timing of each refuelling bracket are not publicly documented in detail, so bracket positions/times here are evenly-reasoned interpolations across the known total outbound time (~8 hours) and known total distance (~3,380 nm), anchored to the few precise data points available (e.g. the documented 34-minute gap between brackets 1 and 2). The route itself is plotted as a true great-circle track between Wideawake Airfield and Stanley Airport — real, precisely-sourced coordinates, not a guess. Section/callsign colouring (Red/White/Blue) is illustrative beyond the two specifically-documented callsigns ("Blue 2" for XM607, "White 2" for XL189).
