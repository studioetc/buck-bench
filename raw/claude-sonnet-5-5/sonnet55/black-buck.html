30 April 1982 · Wideawake, Ascension Island

OPERATION BLACK BUCK

Britain’s long-range answer to Port Stanley’s runway was a 1950s V-bomber flying from the middle of the Atlantic — some 3,400 nautical miles each way, with nowhere to land and nowhere to refuel but in the air. The Vulcan could only get there because eleven Victor tankers passed fuel to each other, and to it, in a bucket-brigade that peeled away one tanker at a time. If any link broke, the chain unravelled.

This is that chain, animated on a real map: every bracket, every transfer, every fuel state — plus the things that went wrong.

Sources & method

What is real, and what is modelled

The refuelling plan

DOCUMENTEDThe bracket latitudes/longitudes, the 15 transfers (7 into the Vulcan, 8 tanker-to-tanker), the quantities passed and the tanker roles come from the RAF’s own refuelling “spaghetti diagram” (the Black Buck II version, which refined Black Buck 1’s plan) published in RAF Historical Society Journal 30. The RAF’s own summary — 11 tankers and 15 transfers to bring Vulcan XM607 to striking distance — matches it exactly.

Black Buck 1 as flown

DOCUMENTEDPrimary Vulcan XM598 (Reeve) loses cabin pressure and returns; XM607 (Withers) takes over · Victor XL163’s hose fault, replaced by reserve XH669 (Biglands) · unforecast thunderstorms near 40°S; Biglands’ probe breaks during the last tanker-to-tanker transfer · Tuxford (XL189) presses on below fuel minima and passes ~7,000 lb less than planned · Withers reaches the Rio rendezvous about an hour late with ~7,000 lb · Seymour is launched at 07:30Z to meet Tuxford “over a thousand miles” out · bombs at 04:38 local (07:38Z), landing 14:52Z. Sources: Wikipedia, the Collectair “Longest Night” account (Tuxford, Biglands, Brooks, Neal, Seymour), the RAF and Vulcan to the Sky.

Modelled — treat as illustrative

  • MODELLED Speeds, climb profiles, exact join-ups, holding patterns, the fuel burn between contacts (calibrated so the Vulcan’s ledger reproduces the plan’s transfer amounts and the “~7,000 lb at the RV” figure) and the Rio RV timeline.
  • INFERRED Which tanker occupied which slot for named crews (Brooks, Neal, Tuxford, Biglands) is inferred from the accounts; the plan labels (R1…B7) are the RAF diagram’s, not Black Buck 1’s own RED/WHITE/BLUE call-signs.
  • MODELLED The fuel Biglands hands back is solved so that Tuxford meets Seymour with about 30 minutes of fuel, as reported. Sources disagree on several distances and times; where they conflict the model picks the figure that keeps the whole timeline consistent.
  • Coastlines: Natural Earth. Day/night shading computed for 1 May 1982. Task Force and Nimrod positions are approximate.

Scale

A Vulcan is 31 m long; a bracket is tens of miles. Icons, trails, formation spacing and hoses are drawn at an exaggerated size so the chain is legible — the exaggeration factor is shown live at the bottom-left of the map. Everything else (where, when, how much fuel) is to scale.

Mission complete · 14:52Z

The longest bombing raid in history

RAF Historical Society figures for the whole operation: 1,955,000 lb (244,000 imp. gal) of fuel uplifted by the Victors; the Vulcan received 7% of it and 20% was transferred between Victors; the fuel passed at the last outbound transfer had passed through five different tankers.
